Cutting AI cost per question by 90% while usage tripled

Our AI bill nearly doubled. The obvious explanation was growth. The real one was a supplier price change, and fixing it properly meant rethinking how the product’s AI was built.

$48 → $5cost per 1,000 questions
29K → 90KAI questions a month
Ownercost analysis, the case for the rebuild, launch testing

The Problem

SuperInvesting answers investing questions about 5,000+ Indian stocks with AI. Every question costs money in model calls, so AI cost is one of our biggest variable costs, and it grows with every user we add.

When AI spend jumped 1.9x, the natural read was “more users, more questions.” Usage was growing, so the story was believable. Accepting it would have meant treating a structural cost problem as a sign of success.

Finding the Real Cause

I own AI cost analysis, so I broke the spend down instead of reading the total. Usage growth didn’t explain the jump. The price per call had gone up: a model provider had raised its prices.

When a cost moves, split it into volume, intensity and price before deciding what the story is.

The Issue Underneath

The price hike exposed something more important. Our AI ran as a 7-agent framework, where several specialised agents handled each question. One user question turned into many model calls, so every upstream price change got multiplied.

The Options

  1. Absorb it. Keep the architecture and accept higher cost per user, which gets worse with growth.
  2. Swap the model only. Quick, but the multiplier stays for the next price change.
  3. Rebuild around one orchestrator with tools. One model decides what to do and calls a stock screener, browser or research tool only when needed. More work, but it removes the multiplier.

The Decision

I made the case for option 3 first, because it was the main lever on cost, then for a model switch on top of it. Doing it in that order meant we didn’t just swap one expensive setup for a cheaper version of the same problem.

Shipping It Safely

A cheaper system is no good if the answers get worse, and in investing a wrong number costs users real money. I ran the launch testing, checking the new system’s answers before release so the savings didn’t come at the expense of quality.

The Result

  • Cost per 1,000 questions fell from $48 to $5, about 90% lower.
  • Over the same period, monthly AI questions grew from 29K to 90K and paid users from 5.4K to 12.8K, driven by the Economic Times partnership.
  • Growth now makes the business healthier instead of just making the AI bill bigger.

What I Took Away

For AI products, cost is a product decision, not just an engineering one. The architecture decides what every question costs, and that decides which growth you can afford. The engineering background helped me see the multiplier. Product made it my job to argue for removing it.
